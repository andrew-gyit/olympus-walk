# 6. Planetary protection and precaution system

## 6.1 Purpose

The original brief proposes an information panel called “Zonas de precaución” to communicate exploration considerations, prevent harmful biological contamination, and protect areas of scientific interest. Olympus Walk should translate these concepts into educational gameplay and map/world overlays.

## 6.2 Four information blocks from the brief

### A. Protect the Martian Environment

Core message: avoid introducing or dispersing terrestrial microorganisms that could contaminate the environment and compromise future research.

Recommended educational measures in the brief:
- Avoid introducing or dispersing terrestrial microorganisms.
- Respect equipment cleaning and contamination-control protocols.
- Avoid unnecessary intervention in areas of astrobiological interest.
- Apply special precautions during sample collection and handling.

### B. Watch for Special Regions

Core message: some areas could present conditions relevant to microbial survival and require special consideration. Uncertain regions should be approached preventively until sufficient evidence is available.

Recommended educational measures in the brief:
- Recognise special regions identified through the applicable scientific framework.
- Consider possible liquid-water sources, geothermal activity, and other relevant characteristics.
- Avoid entry or intervention without appropriate scientific assessment and operational authorisation.
- Treat uncertain regions preventively.

### C. Assess the Terrain Before Moving

Core message: assess slopes, elevation changes, rough terrain, depressions, obstacles, and possible ice/brine indicators before choosing a route. The brief explicitly cautions that possible deposits do not by themselves confirm water or life.

### D. Follow Mission Protection Measures

Core message: review scientific evaluations and mission planetary-protection procedures before entering sensitive areas; verify the route and stops; monitor alerts; update route assessment when risks are found; record relevant scientific observations.

## 6.3 Precaution categories

A single location may have multiple categories.

### Topographical precaution
- Steep slopes.
- Elevation changes.
- Rugged or difficult terrain.
- Obstacles and depressions.

### Environmental precaution
- Extreme temperatures.
- Ice or possible water-related deposits.
- Surface/subsurface characteristics.
- Environmental conditions requiring scientific assessment.

### Planetary-protection precaution
- Special regions.
- Uncertain regions.
- Possible liquid-water environments.
- Geological features of astrobiological interest.
- Risk of biological contamination.

## 6.4 Gameplay integration

The system can:
- Display a warning when approaching a precaution polygon.
- Show the category and reason.
- Explain recommended behaviour.
- Require acknowledgement or an alternate route in a training mission.
- Record the player's decision in the mission log.
- Adjust an educational protocol-compliance score.

Do not turn the educational scoring mechanic into a legal judgment. The game should explain concepts, not issue legal determinations.

## 6.5 Evidence and legal/scientific distinction

The original brief references the 1967 Outer Space Treaty, Article IX, COSPAR's 2020 Planetary Protection Policy, NASA planetary-protection material, UN guidance, the Artemis Accords, and other space-law instruments. The game documentation should distinguish binding treaty obligations from policies, technical standards, guidelines, and recommendations. Any legal summary intended for publication should be checked against the current official text and the mission context.

## 6.6 Geography and uncertainty

Do not draw fictional training polygons and label them as officially designated Martian special regions. Every precaution area must expose one of the following statuses:
- Verified source geometry.
- Derived from documented data/criteria.
- Fictional training scenario.
- Uncertain / requires expert assessment.

A warning may be appropriate even when a region is uncertain, but the interface must not imply that uncertainty equals confirmed danger or confirmed habitability.
